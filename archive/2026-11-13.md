### 1. [Back to Pixels: Lifting Latent Video Diffusion into Pixel Space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17336v1)

- 来源：arXiv
- 关键词：video generation, latent video, pixel space., diffusion, transformer, count keeps depth-conditioned video
- 链接：<https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17336v1>

**原始摘要**

Frontier video generation models work in a VAE latent space that compresses video in both space and time. This compression made training tractable, but now limits high-resolution, controllable, and interactive generation -- doubling resolution quadruples token count, conditions apply to groups of frames rather than individual ones, and each response must wait for the rest of its multi-frame block. We present PixVid, a simple yet effective method that lifts a pretrained latent video model to directly generate every frame in pixel space. PixVid keeps the diffusion transformer and replaces the VAE decoder with a convolutional head that renders its outputs into pixels. We find that predicting clean video rather than velocity, together with a larger noise shift, is key to quality. Finetuned from Wan2.1-14B, PixVid is competitive with the latent baseline within $\sim 1.3$M training clips. Working framewise in pixels, it generates at $2\times$ higher resolution with the same per-frame token count, keeps depth-conditioned video editing aligned under fast motion, and responds to each action at the next frame. ### 2. [K2V: Generative Keyframing For Physically Plausible Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17324v1)

- 来源：arXiv
- 关键词：video generation, diffusion, reasoning, benchmark, model-backed keyframe generation module, language model-backed keyframe generation
- 链接：<https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17324v1>

**原始摘要**

Video diffusion models are able to generate samples with high-quality appearance and textural qualities. However, content produced by these methods is known to exhibit significant physical errors. This is because, in a classical video generation setting, all physical reasoning must take place simultaneously and exclusively within the diffusion process.We propose to resolve these issues by redistributing the task of physical reasoning onto a language model-backed keyframe generation module, which allows for explicit reasoning in language, and fine-grained control over the appearance of the video at important moments in the video. Results demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms existing approaches, achieving state-of-the-art performance on the Physics-IQ Verified benchmark and PhyGenBench.

### 3. [Learning When Diffusion Language Models Should Regenerate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17320v1)

- 来源：arXiv
- 关键词：reinforcement learning, diffusion language, reasoning, fine-tuning, knowing WHEN and WHAT, WHEN the surrounding context
- 链接：<https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17320v1>

**原始摘要**

Remasking allows diffusion language models to revisit generated text, but effective correction requires knowing when and what to revise. Our diagnosis on Sudoku reveals two gaps. Hidden states contain error information that token confidence does not reliably expose, and remasking an isolated error usually reproduces it when the surrounding context remains fixed. These findings motivate DiVR , a verifier that predicts whether a partially generated sequence will reach a correct answer. Trained on the final correctness of the model's own trajectories, DiVR decides when to regenerate a block and which alternative to retain, while the generator itself stays unchanged. On five reasoning benchmarks with LLaDA-8B-Base, DiVR raises the average accuracy of the fine-tuned generator from 35.3\% to 43.4\% at $1.9$-$3.5\times$ the cost of greedy decoding, above search baselines that use $3.8$-$7.4\times$. It also complements post-training such as supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, and generalizes to other generators, including Dream.

### 6. [eDMD: Endpoint Distribution Matching for Few-Step Diffusion Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17194v1)

- 来源：arXiv
- 关键词：distribution matching, distillation, endpoint distribution, diffusion, qwen-image-2.1 demonstrate that EDMD, matching for few-step
- 链接：<https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17194v1>

**原始摘要**

Distribution matching distillation (DMD) has emerged as a leading paradigm for distilling diffusion models into few-step students, where an auxiliary fake score model tracks the student's distribution and the discrepancy between the teacher and fake predictions drives the student toward the teacher. In multi-step distillation, although only the student's final output is delivered, existing methods train the fake model on intermediate states or across all denoising steps. Consequently, the student's update signal becomes entangled with intermediate distributions, which can distort the matching direction and remain nonzero even after the student's final outputs already match the teacher distribution. To resolve this mismatch, we propose Endpoint Distribution Matching Distillation (eDMD), which trains a single shared fake model exclusively on the student's final outputs and uses it to correct every denoising step. Our theoretical analysis proves that this endpoint reference guarantees that the distribution matching term vanishes if and only if the student's final output distribution matches the teacher's, regardless of intermediate distributions. Beyond the fake reference, we further strengthen teacher supervision through a precise teacher (PT) that replaces single-step guided predictions with short multi-step trajectories on the final noise interval, paired with a calibrated query density for intermediate steps. Experiments on SD3-Medium, FLUX.2-klein (4B and 9B), and Qwen-Image-2.1 demonstrate that eDMD consistently outperforms existing distribution matching methods for 4-step generation.

### 8. [Inner Product Regularization for Imitation Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17159v1)

- 来源：arXiv
- 关键词：inner product, product regularization, diffusion, state perturbations, common NULL space. inner, inner raises the average
- 链接：<https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17159v1>

**原始摘要**

Robots that learn from demonstrations should extrapolate their policy despite state perturbations such as sensor noise, scene variation, and compounding errors. One approach to this robustness is regularization. Prior regularizers consider each state on its own and limit how sensitive the policy is to any perturbation at that state. By contrast, we explore how the policy's output shifts when given the same perturbation at different states. Our insight is that, locally, the robot should respond to state perturbations in a consistent manner: e.g., if changing an irrelevant object position does not impact the policy at one state, that same change should not shift the policy output at a nearby state.
