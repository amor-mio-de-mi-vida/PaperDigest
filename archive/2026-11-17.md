**原始摘要**

Yet most Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies still rely on much smaller VLM backbones, leaving this potential largely untapped. ### 2. [Reference Forcing: Long Video Generation with Region-Guided Drift Correction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21559v1)

- 来源：arXiv
- 关键词：video generation, reference forcing, LONG video, subject and background, attention, distillation
- 链接：<https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21559v1>

**原始摘要**

Long video generation should keep the subject and background consistent while supporting rich, natural motion. However, autoregressive models are often distilled with distribution matching distillation (DMD) over short windows, which does not train them to keep this consistency over long ranges. As a result, the subject and background can drift over time. Methods that manage the key-value (KV) cache retain or retrieve history to reduce drift. Yet this history may itself have drifted. We propose Reference Forcing, which uses the initial frames as an appearance reference. We first build a teacher that uses attention masks to force each region of a new frame to attend to the same region in the initial frames. The teacher finds these regions with off-the-shelf segmentation models. We then distill this behavior into a separate attention path of the student, which needs no external models at inference. We further introduce value correction, which corrects drifted values in the KV cache toward the matching values of drift-free initial frames. Experiments show that Reference Forcing achieves state-of-the-art subject and background consistency among long-video methods on different base models, and its advantage grows with video length. Ablations show both components are needed to gain consistency without losing motion. More visualizations and code are available at https://zhaosheng-thu.github.io/reference-force/

### 3. [Lazy-MOSAIC: Accelerating Skill-Centric Manipulation Planning with Learned Skill-Effect World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21517v1)

- 关键词：learned skill-effect world, world models, high-fidelity world model SUCH, produces. lazy-mosaic THEN USES, skill-effect world model predicts, planning with learned skill-effect
- 链接：<https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21517v1>

**原始摘要**

MOSAIC, a recent skill-centric approach, lets the skills guide a non-sequential search toward the regions of this space where they are likely to succeed; however, it pays the full cost of simulation for every parameterized skill it considers. In this work, we introduce Lazy-MOSAIC, a lazy planning framework that instead searches over hypothesized skill outcomes. A cheap learned skill-effect world model predicts, in a fraction of a millisecond, a skill's feasibility and the world state it produces. Lazy-MOSAIC then uses these predictions to focus expensive simulation on promising candidate skills and, when a hypothesis is invalidated, repairs only the affected part of the search, while ensuring that every skill in a returned plan is validated in simulation. We evaluate Lazy-MOSAIC on cluttered-shelf retrieval tasks of increasing difficulty and demonstrate it on a physical robot, showing that it plans substantially faster than baselines with fewer simulator queries, while matching or exceeding their success rate in every setting. ### 5. [Variationally correct physics-informed operator learning for nonlinear partial differential equations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21469v1)

- 来源：arXiv
- 关键词：physics-informed operator learning, partial differential, variationally correct, finite element, diffusion, pde-compliant norm. for nonlinear
- 链接：<https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21469v1>

**原始摘要**

Physics-informed operator learning trains neural operators on the residual of a parametric partial differential equation (PDE) instead of labeled solution data, but its reliability depends on a variationally correct residual: one that is equivalent to the error in a norm dictated by the equation, the PDE-compliant norm. For nonlinear equations written as first-order systems, we prove that the first-order system least-squares residual is equivalent to the error in this norm near a solution branch if the linearized residual is stable and the residual derivative is Lipschitz continuous, possibly in a norm stronger than the error norm. We verify these conditions for monotone reaction, solution- and gradient-dependent diffusion, and compressible Neo-Hookean hyperelasticity. The stability mechanisms vary across these classes, as do the regions where the equivalence holds, from arbitrary bounded sets to a neighborhood of the branch. In a conforming finite element space, we show that the loss excess of a state near the finite element solution is equivalent to its squared distance from that solution. We use this loss to train a reduced basis neural operator with values in this space, with solution snapshots needed only for the basis and the loss and its symbolically derived gradient assembled by the finite element library. At inference, this loss estimates the error. Numerically, the square root of the loss excess tracks the error within factors comparable to those of the linear case, and the operator achieves higher accuracy in the PDE-compliant norm than grid-based baselines evaluated in the same finite element space.

### 6. [Generative Data Augmentation Without External Data: An Empirical and Theoretical Study on Deep Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21467v1)

- 来源：arXiv
- 关键词：synthetic data HELP, generative data augmentation, without external, ASK WHEN and WHY, SET and ASK WHEN, WHEN and WHY adding
- 链接：<https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21467v1>

**原始摘要**

Generative data augmentation adds samples from generative models to a classifier's training set. Many influential studies use generators pre-trained on large external datasets, and so mix the value of synthetic data with knowledge transferred through pretraining. We study the case without external data, where each generator learns only from the classifier's own training set, and ask when and why adding generated samples to that training set raises or lowers the classifier's test accuracy. We train classifiers with four backbones on six datasets, two of them medical, adding samples from four generators. We bound the excess risk of a classifier trained with synthetic data, that is, how far its population risk can lie above the minimum risk within its hypothesis class. The bound charges synthetic data for the total variation distance between the real and generated distributions and credits synthetic data for a larger training set. The gain from adding synthetic data, the fall in population risk, is at most the excess risk of the real-only classifier, which trains on the real images alone. Read through the bound, the experiments suggest that synthetic data help most when our classifiers detect only a small discrepancy between the generated and real data and the real-only classifier has room to improve, and that synthetic data help little or hurt when the detected discrepancy is large. Generators with a larger lower bound on the total variation distance, a bound that we estimate from our own classifiers, tend to help less on five of the six datasets, and on the CIFAR datasets the most accurate backbone gains the least. The bound gives these directions but not the size of the largest gains, most of which traditional augmentation also reaches at the same number of optimizer steps.
